		\HRule \\[0.1cm] 	
This thesis documents the design and development of the \textbf{``Drishti'' Wheat Grain Analyser}, an automated device for wheat grain quality assessment at FMCG procurement warehouses. Manual assessment of a 100-gram wheat sample, comprising approximately 2,800 grain entities across twelve categories, takes up to sixty minutes per cycle. This creates logistical bottlenecks and introduces inconsistency due to operator fatigue and uneven expertise.

The research began with contextual inquiry at procurement warehouses and processing factories. Observation and stakeholder interviews identified four design constraints: assessment latency, dependence on tacit expertise, environmental degradation of inspection quality, and susceptibility to adulteration.

The central finding is that the primary barrier to automated grain analysis is physical, not computational. In a dense grain sample, most grain surfaces are occluded. A single overhead image does not contain enough information for reliable classification. This problem is addressed through a hardware-software co-design approach. An electromagnetic solenoid actuation system, using a staggered asynchronous pulse protocol with 50-millisecond inter-solenoid delays, disperses and rotates the grain bed across multiple frames. A precision-milled hole-fitting tray mechanism ensures direct energy transfer from solenoid to grain.

The computational pipeline pairs this mechanical system with a domain-specific machine learning model, trained on images captured under the device's own calibrated illumination. A FastAPI-based server-client architecture, containerised via Docker, delivers sub-five-minute assessment across multiple factory sites.

The device was validated by wheat grain quality experts at FMCG procurement sites. Expert evaluation confirmed classification performance and the suitability of the interface for high-pressure warehouse conditions. Multi-site field trials are planned as future work.
