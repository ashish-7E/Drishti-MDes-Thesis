\section{Summary of Findings}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This thesis addressed a specific operational problem: the 60-minute manual quality assessment cycle in FMCG wheat procurement. The central finding is that the barrier is physical, not computational. A dense 100-gram grain sample cannot be reliably classified from a single overhead image because most grain surfaces are occluded. No model can classify what it cannot see.

Three findings support this:

\begin{itemize}
    \item The high-density packing of approximately 2,800 grain entities per sample creates occlusion that software alone cannot resolve. Any system that does not address this physically will produce biased estimates, over-representing whatever happens to land on the surface.
    \item Staggered solenoid actuation ($T_A$ through $T_D$ at 50\,ms offsets) provides effective grain dispersion and orientation diversity. Vibration, the more obvious alternative, does not, due to energy transfer inefficiency, size-based segregation, and limited rotational excitation.
    \item The hardware-software co-design approach, which involves engineering physical observability to support tractable inference, enabled a cycle time reduction from 60 minutes to under 5 minutes while producing outputs that expert assessors judged consistent with their own.
\end{itemize}

\section{Contribution to Industrial Design Practice}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Two transferable design patterns emerge from this research.

The first is \textbf{Active Vision}: the deliberate engineering of physical conditions to improve the quality of visual information entering an inference system \cite{Aloimonos1988}. In Drishti, the solenoid actuation, dual-illumination optics, fixed white-balance calibration, and multi-frame capture strategy form a single integrated strategy for presenting the model with a tractable problem. This pattern applies to any inspection context where the natural presentation of the subject is unfavourable for the imaging geometry.

The second is \textbf{Human-Centred AI at decision interfaces}: automation applied where it is most reliable, and human judgement preserved where it is most valuable \cite{Shneiderman2020}. The Admin Review mechanism holds near-threshold results for expert consideration. This is not a limitation of the system's accuracy; it reflects the recognition that some procurement decisions carry consequences that require a higher confidence threshold than routine clearances.

\section{Limitations and Future Work}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The Bangalore validation was an expert demonstration, not a longitudinal deployment. It established that the device worked as intended and that outputs were plausible to experienced evaluators. It did not establish performance across the seasonal and regional variation that characterises full-scale FMCG procurement. That requires the multi-site field trials scheduled after the time of writing.

The training dataset, while domain-specific and acquisition-matched, was finite in defect diversity. Edge-case presentations, such as unusual adulteration patterns, atypical damage signatures, and wheat varieties not yet in training, may produce lower-confidence or incorrect classifications at scale.

Three directions for future work are identified:

\textbf{Large-scale field validation:} Complete multi-site trials to characterise the error distribution and identify failure modes that warrant targeted model refinement.

\textbf{Extension to adjacent commodities:} The hardware-software co-design framework is not wheat-specific. Rice, pulses, coffee beans, and spices share dense-entity inspection problems with orientation-sensitive quality attributes. The platform's architecture could be adapted with commodity-specific datasets and threshold calibration.

\textbf{Predictive quality analytics:} Sustained multi-site deployment will generate a longitudinal record of assessment results. Patterns across vendors, seasons, and storage conditions could support predictive procurement risk models beyond individual accept/reject decisions.

%----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Closing Reflection}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Experienced wheat assessors read subtle morphological cues from thousands of grain entities under time pressure, in difficult conditions, with judgements that hold up against chemical analysis. This expertise is rare and not robust to shift length or staff turnover. Drishti was not designed to replace it. The device makes consistent, reliable quality assessment available at more points than human expertise alone can sustain. Whether the device is trustworthy enough to underpin procurement decisions across a full season is a question the field trials will answer.
