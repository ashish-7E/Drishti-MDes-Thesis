The research team visited wheat grain quality experts at ITC Limited's procurement warehouses and processing factories to evaluate the high-fidelity prototype. Food analysts and quality managers observed the device operating on real wheat samples and provided structured qualitative feedback. This evaluation confirmed that the device behaved as intended and that outputs were judged plausible by experienced assessors. It did not establish statistical confidence bounds on classification accuracy across the full range of seasonal and regional procurement conditions. A second validation phase across five factory sites is planned following the June procurement season.

\section{Assessment Latency: Quantitative Comparison}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Manual assessment of a 100-gram wheat sample requires approximately 60 minutes. Drishti reduces this substantially, with the duration depending on the inference hardware deployed.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.95\textwidth]{Images/time_comparison.png}
    \caption{Comparison of assessment times between manual human inspection and the automated Drishti device across various GPU server configurations.}
    \label{fig:time_comparison}
\end{figure}

As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:time_comparison}, a baseline deployment using an NVIDIA RTX 3060 (24GB Server) completes the full assessment in approximately 5 minutes. An NVIDIA RTX 4080 (24GB Server) reduces this to 3 minutes. An NVIDIA RTX 4090 (64GB Server) processes the full pipeline in 1 minute. This reduction restructures procurement logistics: a lot that previously required one hour per assessment can be cleared before the truck queue grows.

Evaluators identified that the staggered-impulse solenoid actuation produces genuine grain rotation, not merely lateral redistribution. This was particularly noted for \textit{Kernel Burnt} specimens: ventral-side entry marks became visible in the image sequence in a way that static loading would not have supported. The hole-fitting tray interface was adopted quickly by all evaluators after a brief demonstration. Dust management was cited explicitly by one quality manager as a practical benefit of the rail-free design.

\section{Classification Performance}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

% Note to author: Insert confusion matrix, per-category precision/recall/F1 scores,
% and comparison against manual assessment baseline when available.

Expert evaluation was conducted on the same wheat lot samples used in the demonstration. Results are indicative, pending full statistical analysis.

Major category detection, including pure wheat, stone and chaff, and premium variety separation, was rated consistent with manual expert readings. The \textit{Sharbati}/\textit{Lokwan} distinction, which junior assessors find difficult, was handled correctly in the reviewed cases. Performance on orientation-sensitive defect categories appeared stronger than evaluators had expected from a single-camera system. Evaluators attributed this to the multi-frame capture approach, confirming that mechanical exposure of occluded grain surfaces improves defect detection.

To evaluate classification consistency, tests were conducted across ten sample batches under three conditions: manual human inspection, an open-environment camera, and the Drishti device.

As summarised in Figure~\ref{fig:accuracy_flowchart}, manual inspection yielded 80\% average accuracy with high variance due to operator differences and fatigue. An open-environment camera averaged 60\% accuracy with medium variance due to shifting ambient lighting. Drishti achieved 98\% average accuracy with low variance, owing to its consistent mechanical dispersion and controlled illumination.

\section{Expert Feedback: Design and Interface \\Evaluation}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Tray operation was rated faster than evaluators had anticipated. The full cycle sequence was comprehensible without explanation. One evaluator noted that the device could be loaded in the time it takes to walk back to the sorting station, a comment that directly reflects the integration concerns identified in fieldwork.

Threshold visualisation was rated more immediately interpretable than the numerical tabular format used in existing warehouse records. Senior managers found the threshold-relative indicators particularly useful for borderline cases. The Admin Review feature for $\pm 2\%$ results received explicit approval. Quality managers described it as consistent with how they already exercise oversight: reviewing borderline calls personally rather than delegating ambiguous decisions.
